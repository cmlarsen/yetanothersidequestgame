# Monetization — landscape survey

> Status: **landscape, not doctrine.** Captured 2026-07. A map of how this
> genre actually makes money and how the obvious levers score against our own
> design laws — nothing here is a business plan. Rung 4 of the goal ladder
> ("pays for itself") is the near-term bar, and that's ~$30–80/month of
> fly.io, not a revenue model.

## How the comps do it

- **Pokémon GO** — free-to-play selling **throughput, not power**: raid
  passes (access to social moments), incubators (speed up progress you'd make
  anyway), item/creature **storage upgrades** (the quiet workhorse —
  inventory-pressure relief), paid event tickets (GO Fest), avatar cosmetics.
  Plus a B2B layer nobody can copy at our scale (sponsored locations).
  Nothing in the shop makes your creatures meaningfully stronger — a decade
  of shop trust rests on that.
- **Zombies, Run!** — sells **content**: subscription unlocking the mission
  catalog. A story game selling its story. Directly relevant: it monetizes
  authored audio/narrative, which is what a solo-dev-plus-Claude pipeline can
  keep producing.
- **Orna** — light cosmetics/QoL purchases; survives fine at indie scale.
- **Gacha / loot boxes** — the dominant mobile model, named only to reject:
  whale-dependent, trust-corrosive, legally radioactive with kids in several
  jurisdictions, and tonally poison for a QfG-hearted game.

## Scoring the obvious levers against our own laws

(Laws in play: never charge for the fun button; collection must feed power;
a shared world must be fair.)

| Lever | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Gating core features | ❌ never for the core verb. Gating *content* is a different, legitimate thing. |
| Pay-to-win gear / buying resources | ❌ in a shared world with a kid's friend group, the rich kid wins and everyone else quits. Fairness is retention, not just ethics. |
| Extra gear space | ✅ proven (PoGO's workhorse). Calibration rule: free space is comfortable; paid space is for hoarders. |
| **Different apprentice / companions** | ✅✅ the sleeper hit. A new sidekick with own voice, snark, quirks, and a *sideways* (not stronger) kit = selling content and attachment, not power. Monetizes what the tone doc says we're best at: characters. **"Sell friends, not power."** |
| Bastion upgrades | Depends: paid power ❌; paid speed-up ⚠️ (the WU energy-gate smell); bastion *cosmetics* ✅ once friends can see each other's camps. |
| Cosmetics generally | ✅ but only worth much once someone else can see them (map-visible friends). Cheap to produce in pixel art. |

## A plausible spine (sketch, not plan)

NORTH-STAR already decided *content ships in seasons* — which makes the
natural monetization spine a **season pass**:

- **Free track:** all mechanics, the shared world, the events. Everyone plays
  the same game.
- **Paid track:** the season's authored questline chapter, a premium
  companion, cosmetics.

Around the spine: storage/QoL as the evergreen trickle; companions à la
carte; cosmetics once friends are visible. And the humblest rung-4 answer
first: a one-time **supporter/founder purchase** from a few dozen early
players covers the server bill before any economy exists.

## Instincts worth keeping in view (still just ideas)

If the audience ends up "adults and kids, PoGO-shaped," these currently feel
right — none of them are rules yet:

- No loot boxes / gacha, ever.
- No FOMO countdown pressure on purchases.
- Real prices in real money — no obfuscating premium-currency laundering.
- The shop passes the test: **"would I be comfortable if my kid's friend's
  parent read this screen?"**
