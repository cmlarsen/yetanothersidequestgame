# Launch — landscape survey

> Status: **landscape, not doctrine.** Captured 2026-07. How location games
> actually reach players, shaped to the NORTH-STAR goal ladder — the ladder
> *is* the launch plan.

## Five truths

1. **Launch is a process, not an event — and it already started.** Rung 1
   (the 13-year-old's friend group) is launch v0. Mechanically: TestFlight
   (free, 10k external testers, weekly builds, no review pressure). Every
   winner in this niche had a long semi-private phase — Orna grew off Reddit
   word-of-mouth for years; Zombies, Run! built its audience pre-ship via a
   Kickstarter that was marketing wearing a funding hat.
2. **Density beats reach.** A shared-world location game with 500 players
   scattered nationally feels dead; 500 in one metro feels alive. Launch in
   concentric circles — friend group → school → town → metro — opening each
   circle only when the previous one *retains* (D7 is the gate, not a date).
   Design corollary: sparse must still feel okay — async traces (a sealed
   tear with someone's name on it, a bounty-board entry) make three players
   in a town feel like a community.
3. **The YouTube channel is the acquisition engine.** *Yet Another Side
   Quest* + "solo dad builds a real-world RPG, his kid's friends playtest
   it" = a devlog series with a built-in arc. Build-in-public is the proven
   zero-budget channel, and location games demo unusually well — the footage
   is the real world. Discord becomes home base when strangers appear.
4. **Store mechanics, when the visible launch comes:** soft launch in a
   small English-speaking market (NZ/AU — the PoGO route) to tune D1/D7
   before the one-shot debut; App Store pre-orders to concentrate day-one
   velocity; pitch Apple editorial ~6–8 weeks ahead (polished novel-category
   solo indie is exactly the featuring profile). Niche press and
   walking-game YouTubers; general press needs a story — "my kid's school is
   obsessed" is one.
5. **The growth engine lives in the game, not the marketing.** The moment
   that matters is a kid texting "get this so we can raid the tear by
   school." Design that invitation; make it the most polished flow in the
   app.

## The invitation: sigils, QR codes, and App Clips

- **App Clips are built for the party-join moment.** Scan a QR → iOS
  launches a <15MB slice of the app instantly — no store visit, no install,
  no account. Flow at the park: kid shows his sigil → friend scans with the
  camera → seconds later the friend sees *this park in the other realm*, in
  the party, watching the fight → full-install upsell comes **after** the
  taste. The recruit happens before the install.
- **The fiction gift-wraps it.** A QR code already looks like a *sigil*.
  Every Warden bears a mark; scanning a friend's mark with your tome binds
  you to their party. Recruit rewards as fictional flavor (trinket,
  cosmetic) rather than power — same instinct as the MONETIZATION notes.
- Platform note: App Clips are iOS-only (Android Instant Apps is the weaker
  analog) — acceptable given iOS-first, and US teens are iPhone-shaped
  anyway.

## Guerrilla layer: posters at PoGO hotspots

Precision targeting for the cost of a laser printer: PoGO gyms/stops are a
pre-filtered audience (proven location-game players, standing at a landmark).

- **The QR must be immediately magical about that exact spot.** Not a
  landing page — an App Clip opening a live view of *this place* torn open
  in the other realm: "There's been an incursion here. See it." Hotspots are
  hotspots because they're landmarks → OSM knows them → deterministic
  procgen can *guarantee* a tear at every one. The poster is a rift marker;
  scanning it is looking through the veil. That's what a PoGO player
  screenshots and posts.
- Practicalities: community boards / library kiosks / game-store windows are
  always safe, utility poles vary by town; light touch around PoGO community
  norms (poster near a gym = fair game; spamming raid meetups = hostility);
  laminate and occasionally re-check posters (QR re-stickering is a known
  prank).

## The gates

Between every circle: retention numbers, not calendar dates. Healthy D7 →
open the next circle. Unhealthy → the launch plan is "fix the game." For the
next several rungs, *launch* and *develop* are the same activity.
